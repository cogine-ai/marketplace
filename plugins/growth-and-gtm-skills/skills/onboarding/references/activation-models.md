# Activation Models

Choose how users reach convincing value in relation to payment before optimizing the steps inside that model. Honor existing product commitments when evaluating a change.

| Model | Useful when | Tradeoff to test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Freemium | A lasting free use case demonstrates value and there is a clear paid use case | Free delivery cost, meaningful upgrade demand, and whether limits preserve promised utility |
| Free trial | The product can demonstrate value within a defined window | Setup time, trial length, and the effect of requiring a card on signups and downstream activation/conversion |
| Paid trial | A smaller paid commitment can support evaluation | Entry friction, value during the trial, and the clarity of subsequent billing |
| Refund-backed purchase | Users can evaluate real value after purchase within the stated terms | Refund rate, trust, support burden, and the actual refund promise |
| Consultation / guided setup | Product complexity or buying process requires human help | Time to value, staff capacity, and whether assistance resolves a real barrier |

Start from the audience, price, buying process, setup effort, and evidence of time to value. Compare the relevant alternatives with signup, activation, paid conversion, retention, and service cost; do not choose from signup rate alone. Set trial duration, price, and refund terms for this product rather than copying a generic benchmark.

After choosing the model, use [minimum-path-to-value.md](minimum-path-to-value.md) to design the flow. Preserve a useful promised free/trial experience and explain what makes paid continuation valuable.
