# Structural Copy Review

Use this when a draft is generic, repetitive, or sounds assembled from templates.
These are editing heuristics adapted from the upstream AI-tells reference, not
a detector of authorship or universal bans. Preserve the user's language,
brand register, accessibility needs, and any style rules already supplied.

## Patterns to inspect

- Repeated contrast reveals ("not X, but Y") that manufacture a false choice.
- Long negation lists that replace an explanation of what the product does.
- Self-answered questions or colon reveals used as a punchline rather than a
  question the buyer would actually ask.
- Trailing clause stacks that bury the main claim after several commas.
- Fragments, three-part lists, mirrored rhythms, or bold labels on every line.
- Empty superlatives and vague intensifiers with no mechanism or evidence.
- Fake vulnerability, invented customer quotes, or unnamed "expert" sources.
- A headline that works unchanged on every competitor's site.

A purposeful contrast, question, emoji, em dash, or three-part list can fit the
brand. Review repetition and clarity in context; do not apply an English word
blacklist or punctuation rules to every language.

## Rewrite from the facts

1. Name the actual action, mechanism, customer, or supported result. Do not
   invent numbers to make vague copy sound specific.
2. Give each sentence one main point and connect it to the next.
3. Run the swap test on the hero and section headings. When the differentiator
   is missing, mark `[NEED: differentiator or proof]` rather than fabricating it.
4. Keep justified uncertainty. A scoped claim is stronger than false certainty.
5. Vary sentence structure and read the result aloud. Choose rhetorical devices
   deliberately instead of replacing one repeated pattern with another.

## Self-check

Check the headline, subhead, CTA, and short posts for vague claims, repeated
contrast/negation patterns, question punchlines, and overloaded sentences.
Review longer sections for repetition and unsupported facts. Apply the user's
voice and brand choices, then show only the issues that matter to the reader.

The [copy-frameworks.md](copy-frameworks.md) formulas are starting points; the
[natural-transitions.md](natural-transitions.md) examples help connect ideas.
Neither supplies facts or predicts conversion uplift.
