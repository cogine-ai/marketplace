# Content Production and Distribution

Plan a source piece and how the audience will find and use it together.

## Create once, adapt for the channel

1. Choose the piece's job and source format: guide, research, video, podcast, or interactive asset.
2. Identify reusable sections before production: a standalone explanation, demonstration, quote, chart, checklist, or short clip. Preserve the source's meaning, context, and attribution in each cut.
3. Match each cut to a channel where the intended audience already participates. A transcript, written summary, or captions can make audiovisual material accessible and reusable.
4. Give each destination an appropriate next action, such as reading the full guide, trying the relevant product workflow, or joining an owned list.

## Own, rent, borrow

Use [launch's ORB framework](../../launch/SKILL.md#the-orb-framework) for channel selection. Borrowed audiences and rented platforms can introduce the work; owned pages and subscriber relationships provide a durable place to continue the interaction. Choose channels by audience evidence and capacity, not a requirement to publish everywhere.

For each piece, record:

| Source | Reusable cut | Channel / audience | Next action | Owner / date | Measure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Guide / video / research | Excerpt / clip / chart | Where it fits | Useful continuation | Accountable person and schedule | Qualified engagement or conversion |

## Promote, learn, maintain

Schedule promotion and appropriate resurfacing, then collect questions and objections. Feed those observations into the next piece. Compare qualified engagement and conversion with the effort required.

Match maintenance to the content: recheck facts, sources, links, product screenshots, and examples; refresh derivative pieces when their source changes. Choose cadence from actual decay and audience response instead of a fixed platform half-life.

Social or email execution can be handed to an available specialist when requested. This plan does not require an unbundled skill, or authorize sending or publishing content.
