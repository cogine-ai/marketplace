# Free Tool Case Benchmarks

Named free-tool patterns. Verify current primary evidence before relying on a historical case; use your own audience, costs, and measured results for forecasts.

## Framing: "Your product is my marketing opportunity"

Bezos's line was **"your margin is my opportunity"** — where a competitor monetizes something, undercut it. The engineering-as-marketing version is **"your product is my marketing opportunity."** Take a capability others sell, build a simple free version, and turn it into an acquisition channel. Unsplash gave away the stock photos that Getty charged for — and Getty ended up acquiring it.

## Case Library

| Tool | Company | Pattern |
|------|---------|---------|
| Unsplash | Crew | Reuse a valuable byproduct |
| Website Grader | HubSpot | Analyze inputs to reveal product fit |
| Forecasting | Baremetrics | Demonstrate an adjacent paid workflow |
| Headline Analyzer | CoSchedule | Encourage useful repeat use |
| Keyword Explorer | Moz | Expose a useful part of the product |
| Salary Calculator | Buffer | Create a shareable resource |
| Hatchful | Shopify | Feed a relevant onboarding workflow |

## What each case teaches

Evaluate whether the tool is adjacent to the product or primarily a reach opportunity, how it earns repeat use, what its inputs reveal about qualification, and whether its next step helps the user continue into a paid workflow. Named examples do not establish expected conversion rates for another product.

## How to use these benchmarks

- **Set expectations**: Analyzer/grader tools tend to convert visitors to leads well and qualify them; calculators skew toward reach and share-worthiness; generators feed onboarding.
- **Justify the build**: Estimate lead value, audience size, maintenance costs, and conversion using evidence from your own context before committing engineering time.
- **Pattern-match**: Find the case closest to your concept (adjacent-to-product vs pure-reach) and borrow its gating and distribution approach.
